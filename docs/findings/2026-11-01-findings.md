# Whale-watch findings

As of November 1, 2026. The goal was a chart showing when institutions enter a US stock over days or weeks, with the buy decision left to the reader.

## Bottom line

Free data did not show that goal can be met. Block-venue activity is real and measurable, but it has no buy or sell side and failed the tests of what it means. The highlighted periods should not be read as whales entering.

One result is worth repeating: across stocks, higher block activity in a quarter went with more net institutional buying. That covers one quarter, and it says nothing about timing inside the quarter.

## Test results

The pass rules for the first set were written down first, in [the pre-registration](2026-10-30-preregistration.md). The tests used 60 stocks that played no part in building the signal, covering 9,684 scored stock-weeks.

| Test | Result | Output |
|------|--------|--------|
| Clean install and numbers reproduce | Pass | README |
| Higher score in weeks with $25M+ insider sales (437 weeks) | Fail: effect -0.03, p 0.58 | [proof_eval](results/proof_eval.txt) |
| Block venues beat random ordinary dark pools | Fail: 41st percentile, needed above the 95th | [proof_eval](results/proof_eval.txt) |
| More trading or bigger moves in the next 4 weeks | Fail: p 0.64 and p 0.99 | [proof_eval](results/proof_eval.txt) |
| Direction label predicts the next 4 weeks | Fail: p 0.50 | [proof_eval](results/proof_eval.txt) |
| Buying after a score of 3+ beats other weeks, 13-week hold | Fail: 50% up against 60% | [proof_buy](results/proof_buy.txt) |
| Highlighted weeks sit lower in the 52-week price range | Fail: 76% against 70%, p 0.96 | [price_check](results/price_check.txt) |
| Largest trades lean to buying at the MSFT low | Fail: see below | [msft_prints](results/msft_prints.txt) |

Insider sales are an imperfect stand-in, because many are routine and may not use block venues. So these tests don't prove the signal is useless. They show nothing supports reading it as whale buying.

### Results after the tests failed

These were found by looking, so treat them as leads, not proof.

- **Institutional buying:** across 78 stocks for April to June 2026, block score and net buying by existing holders had a rank correlation of +0.50. It beat all 200 random dark-pool sets. The same check on July to September is still uninformative because only early filers have reported.
- **Periods starting near a low:** 71 periods had a median 13-week return of +9.4% and 68% were up, against +4.1% and 59% for any week in that zone. Price is only a check here and is not part of any score.

### Adding the dollar score

Scoring block dollars as well as block share highlighted more of the weeks when the price moved 10% or more, in either direction. It also made the signal slightly worse on the other measures.

| | Weeks highlighted | 10%+ price-move weeks caught | Up after 13 weeks | 13F correlation (60 stocks) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Block share only | 6.1% | 5% | 65% | 0.47 |
| Share or dollars | 9.5% | 17% | 61% | 0.37 |

Any week for the same stocks was up 60% of the time. See [old_vs_new](results/old_vs_new.txt) and [dollar_test](results/dollar_test.txt).

## MSFT large trades

This test used full-market Alpaca trades for MSFT. The pre-set rule was that net buying must be positive at the June low and better than at the October high. Direction is inferred by comparing each block's price with the last normal on-exchange price, so it is a lean, not a fact.

| | Trading days | Blocks | Block dollars | Buy lean | Sell lean | Mid | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low, June 15 to 30 | 10 | 25 | $722M | $224M | $265M | $233M | -$42M |
| High, October 5 to 16 | 10 | 27 | $792M | $232M | $421M | $139M | -$189M |

The test failed: net was negative at the low, and the gap between the windows had a shuffle p of 0.42. One $328M block on October 9 drives most of that gap. June 23 is missing because its download failed.

A shortcut using each day's 20 most unusual one-minute bars caught 82% of blocks and 91% of block dollars. Its daily measure correlated only 0.31 with block dollars over 20 days. Scanning those minutes for a year would need about 5,020 requests, or 25 minutes per stock the first time at Alpaca's 200 requests a minute. It wasn't built, because the signal failed.

## Free tools

[The full report](../../reports/Free%20whale%20tracking%20tools.md) checked what free sites offer today. None shows institutions entering during a specific span of days or weeks with a real buy or sell side. Filings name the buyer but only to the quarter, and up to about 138 days late. Daily data is fresher but anonymous, so any direction shown is inferred.

Insider filings are the closest exception, with real dates and prices, but they only cover insiders and holders above 10%.

## Next step

Not started. The proposal is a filings chart: shade the quarters when named institutions raised their shares, on a price line, and list who bought. It would replace the highlights on the current chart.

1. Check whether [HedgeFollow](https://hedgefollow.com/stocks/MSFT) shows buy price ranges for free in a normal browser. If it does, cut the build down to what it lacks.
2. Build from the SEC's free 13F data. Automated SEC requests need a contact, and none is set up yet.
3. Try it on MSFT Q2 2026, which contains the late-June low.
4. Write the pass or fail rule first, then test it on Q3 2026 once filings are in after November 16, 2026. Shaded quarters must sit lower in the 52-week price range than unshaded ones.

The window would be about 13 weeks, and filings arrive up to about 45 days after a quarter ends. This would look back, not catch an entry as it happens.

## Files

- `results/` holds saved script output, rerun from local caches on November 1, 2026. `proof_eval.txt` and `proof_buy.txt` are from the original October run. The list of individual Alpaca trades is left out of `msft_prints.txt`.
- `scripts/` holds the scripts as they were run. They are a record, not maintained tools. They read local data caches that aren't committed, because the caches are large and hold raw market data. To rerun them, start from the repo root with the caches present.
- `fetch_proof.py`, `proof_buy.py` and `holdings_test.py` send a browser-style `User-Agent` to OpenInsider, Yahoo and Nasdaq, as does `fetch_holdings` in `whale_watch.py`. These are unofficial endpoints and may stop working.
