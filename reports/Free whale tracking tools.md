# Free whale tools show who or when

Yes, functional free tools exist, but none does exactly what you intended: no free tool verified here shows institutions entering a stock during a specific span of days or weeks with a real buy or sell side. Free data splits in two. Filings name the buyer but place institutional buying only somewhere inside a quarter, and can arrive about 138 days after the trade. Daily dark pool, short volume and options data are fresh but anonymous, and every buy or sell label on them is a website's inference. Form 4 is the near-exception, with name, side, date and real price within two business days, but it covers only company insiders and holders above 10%. For seeing a period when big players entered at relatively low prices, the best fit is a filings-first routine rather than one site: Dataroma's per-manager history for who added and in which quarter, 13F.info for managers Dataroma skips, any free chart for that quarter's high and low, and SECForm4 for dated real prices. That gives you a quarter-long price bracket, not a precise entry. All checks were made on 2026-10-31 from logged-out, non-browser fetchers with no accounts created, so free-account views and most charts remain unconfirmed. Nothing here is a buy or sell recommendation.

## Only Form 4 ties a name, side, date and price together

Four questions decide whether a source can answer yours: who traded, on which side, when, and at what price. The table scores each data type on those questions, and it explains most of what follows.

| Data type | Names the player? | Real buy or sell? | Dates the entry? | Gives a price? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Form 4 (officers, directors, holders above 10%) | Yes | Yes, codes P and S | Yes, filed within 2 business days | Yes, actual price |
| Schedule 13D (control-minded holders above 5%) | Yes | Yes, lists trades | Yes, past 60 days at most | Yes, per share |
| 13F (managers above $100M) | Yes | Net change between quarters, longs only | Quarter only | No |
| FINRA short and off-exchange volume | No | No | Daily or weekly | No |
| Dark pool prints | No | Inferred from the quote | Per trade, sometimes reported late | Yes, print price |
| Options flow | No | Inferred from the quote | Per trade, little free history | Option price, not a stock entry |
| Chart indicators and zones | No | No, inferred from price bars | Any bar | Yes, but tied to no player |

The gaps come from the data, not the websites. FINRA's daily file contains only date, symbol, short volume, short exempt volume, total volume and market ([FINRA file](https://cdn.finra.org/equity/regsho/daily/CNMSshvol20261030.txt)), and 13F reports positions with no trade dates or prices, due 45 days after quarter-end ([SEC FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f)). Schedule 13D is the under-used exception for large non-insiders. It is due within five business days of crossing 5% ([SEC fact sheet](https://www.sec.gov/files/33-11253-fact-sheet.pdf)), and it must describe transactions from the past 60 days with date, amount and price ([17 CFR 240.13d-101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.13d-101)). It applies narrowly, though: qualified institutions and passive investors file 13G instead.

Your own scanner sits in the "off-exchange volume" row. Its highlighted weeks had a median position of 76% of the one-year price range against 70% for other weeks, and block data has no buy or sell side ([whale-watch README](../README.md)). A website republishing the same data type cannot fix that. The README's one encouraging result, a +0.50 rank correlation between block score and net institutional buying across 78 stocks, covers a single quarter and says nothing about timing, which is the part you are after.

The quarter is a hard floor on precision. A purchase on July 1 stays hidden until the November 16, 2026 deadline ([SEC FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f)), which is **138 days**. Shorts and written options are not reported, and confidential treatment can delay a position further. As of today, **2026 Q3 is partial**: 13F.info shows 2,982 filings against 6,672 for Q2 ([13F.info](https://13f.info/cusip/594918104)). Treat Q2 as the last complete quarter until mid-November.

Headline "net institutional buying" is the least trustworthy number in this group. For MSFT, Fintel shows net **+184.0M** shares ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/so/us/msft)), Yahoo shows **-710.9M** ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MSFT/insider-transactions/)), and 13F.info jumps from 5.39B to 9.50B total shares ([13F.info](https://13f.info/cusip/594918104)), more than the roughly 7.43B outstanding implied by Fintel's and Yahoo's percentages. MarketBeat's "12-month" flows exactly match the totals its own FAQ gives for 24 months ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/MSFT/institutional-ownership/)). Vanguard's 2026 split into two advisers is a plausible cause of some of this ([Cooley](https://governancebeat.cooley.com/vanguard-will-restructure-into-two-distinct-investment-advisors/)), because a renamed holder can look like an exit plus a new position. It could also explain the one filer your README excluded for appearing as a new holder across many stocks, though that is unconfirmed inference. Per-manager rows avoid most of this noise.

Stockgrid, the name most older lists recommend, now redirects to AXLFI. Its chart of price, off-exchange volume and 20-session net position is the closest match to your idea in layout, and it is gated ([AXLFI](https://axlfi.com/darkpools)). Its direction rule also looks weak on a plain check. MSFT's FINRA short share ran from 31.3% to 41.4% across October 26 to 30, so the rule reads every day as "selling", including October 29 when volume roughly doubled ([FINRA file](https://cdn.finra.org/equity/regsho/daily/CNMSshvol20261030.txt)). That label likely reflects how the stock normally prints, since ChartExchange's 30-day average is 38.74% ([ChartExchange](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nasdaq-msft/short-volume/)).

FINRA warns that facilitated customer sales appear as short sales, which inflates the ratio, and that site figures are often proprietary calculations ([FINRA notice](https://www.finra.org/industry/information-notice-051019), [FINRA investor page](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/short-interest)). ChartExchange's figures for October 30 do not reconcile to FINRA's file. The "short means buying" idea rests on a vendor's same-day correlation ([SqueezeMetrics](https://squeezemetrics.com/monitor/download/pdf/short_is_long.pdf)), while peer-reviewed work finds sustained shorting flows predicted **negative** returns and short-term spikes predicted nothing ([Wang, Yan and Zheng](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Short_Final_Version.pdf)).

"Dark pool volume" also overstates the whale content. ATS venues were only **35% to 41%** of MSFT's off-exchange shares in the four weeks sampled, with average ATS trades of **43 to 49 shares** ([FINRA API](https://api.finra.org/data/group/otcMarket/name/weeklySummary)). Institutions slice orders, so small prints neither prove nor exclude a large parent order, and one study found both very large and very small trades signal institutional activity ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11439)). Size filters are a weak proxy for "whale". Among the free options, off-exchange volume measured against the stock's own average and shown beside price is the most defensible daily view, as long as you make no direction claim.

Options answer your question poorly for two reasons. Free lookback is thin, so the most a free user can ask is whether daily volume or open interest jumped on some date. And no free tool shows whether a trade opened or closed a position, which even the research data treats as decisive: signals from public quote data predicted returns for only 1 or 2 trading days before reversing, while the strong result needed non-public open-buy data from 1990 to 2001 ([Pan and Poteshman](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10925/w10925.pdf)). Bid/ask signing was about 80% accurate on old CBOE data ([Savickas and Wilson](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v38y2003i04p881-902_00.html)), and a 2-trading-day delay would use up the whole public-signal window in that study. Paid-only products drop out entirely: [FlowAlgo](https://www.flowalgo.com/) has paused sign-ups, [BlackBoxStocks](https://blackboxstocks.com/pricing/) lists no free tier, and [Cheddar Flow](https://www.cheddarflow.com/pricing/) offers a single 7-day trial.

This is the one category that looks like what you asked for, and it is the one to trust least. Every indicator with a disclosed method here, including OBV, Accumulation/Distribution, Chaikin Money Flow and Volume Profile, is computed from public bars, so "accumulation" is an interpretation rather than an observation. The zone scripts confirm this. Zeiierman's zones use a staircase of higher lows with volume only for an in-zone profile ([Zeiierman](https://www.tradingview.com/script/xPd1d97H-Accumulation-And-Distribution-Zones-Zeiierman/)), and UAlgo's fire when volatility drops ([UAlgo](https://www.tradingview.com/script/3k0H6Mbx-Accumulation-Zone-Profiles-UAlgo/)). A low-volatility rule has no direction test, and a zone drawn after its pattern completes starts earlier on the chart than anyone could have known in real time. Hindsight therefore makes these zones look better than they are, because failed zones draw no attention. Wyckoff's "Composite Man", which many of these labels borrow, is itself a heuristic device ([StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)). This research found no study validating any of these tools against real institutional holdings or trades.

## Dataroma plus Form 4 best fits low-price entry periods

The closest free fit to "a period when big players entered at relatively low prices" is **Dataroma's per-manager history**, because it is the only verified no-login page that shows a named manager's quarterly share change beside a reported price. It wins on trustworthiness rather than convenience. Start on a stock's Dataroma page, filter to Buys, and open each manager's history. For managers Dataroma does not track, use 13F.info's manager-by-stock history. Take the quarter where shares rose and read that quarter's high and low on any free chart. That bracket is the widest plausible range for the added shares, assuming the manager held throughout. Compare it to the stock's multi-year range and decide for yourself whether it counts as low.

Then tighten the picture where the data allows. SECForm4 shows dated, real-price insider trades; filter to code P, because awards (A) and tax withholding (F) are not market decisions ([SEC bulletin](https://www.sec.gov/files/forms-3-4-5.pdf)). If a large holder has filed a 13D, its 60-day trade list is the only place a non-insider's dated prices appear. TradingView's FINRA panel can add daily activity context, with no side implied.

This routine falls short of your stated window. It resolves to a quarter of about 13 weeks, not days. It covers net change only, since a manager can buy and sell inside a quarter and show nothing. It picks up index funds whose changes reflect fund flows. And a "reported price" or "average price" from any 13F tool is modeled, not paid; WhaleWisdom says plainly that the fund could have bought at any point in the quarter ([WhaleWisdom FAQ](https://whalewisdom.com/info/faq)).

Research on real institutional behavior also complicates a clean "low-price window". Daily institutional trades estimated from tape data are highly persistent, respond negatively to longer-run past returns, respond positively to recent daily returns, and tend to lose money in the short term before paying off over longer horizons ([Campbell, Ramadorai and Schwartz](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v92y2009i1p66-91.html)). That fits buying after long declines, but it suggests entries spread over time and often look early, not a neat dip. No free tool publishes those estimates per stock, because the method needs trade-level NYSE TAQ data. On signal strength, evidence favors non-routine insider purchases over 13F copying ([Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037e-2b77-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content), [Verbeek and Wang](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v37y2013i9p3454-3471.html)), though those samples are old and show nothing about today.

Several open questions need only a normal browser and a few minutes. If HedgeFollow's buy-price range and average-price chart load without payment, it replaces Dataroma as the best single page, since it would add the price bracket automatically. Fintel's ownership-versus-price chart is the next to check, then WhaleWisdom's stock page, then whether AXLFI's free visit is usable and whether Unusual Whales shows dark pool prints on a $0 account.

## Conclusion

What exists today is not a better version of your highlighted periods, and a better volume threshold will not produce one. Everything fresh enough for a days-or-weeks window has lost the trader's identity, and everything that keeps the identity has lost the timing. That trade-off is set by what regulators publish, so paying for the same public data buys speed and history, not identity. A "whale" label is an inference on top of that data, though true open/close options data does exist at [$850 a month and up](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/ise/2024/34-101096-ex5.pdf).

The real gap in the free market is narrower than it first looks. No verified free site draws filings directly onto price: shading the quarter in which named managers' share counts rose, with that quarter's high and low as a labeled bracket. The inputs are free in the [SEC's 13F data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets). Such a highlight would claim less than "big players are active now", but it could be checked, because each band would point to named filers. The 2026 Q3 filings due November 16 are the natural first test, since that is when "latest quarter" views become complete.
